Regulatory standards compared

Only the United States sets binding, published accuracy criteria for CGMs: the FDA iCGM special controls. CE marking (EU and UK) has no CGM-specific accuracy criteria. The TGA reviews device-specific clinical data but publishes no CGM threshold. eCGM and the IFCC guideline are expert proposals, not law.

As of 17 October 2026. Each row links its source. Health Canada, Medsafe and Asian regulators are not yet reviewed.

4. What this means in practice

  • A CE mark alone says little about accuracy. For a CE-only device, look for a published, peer-reviewed accuracy study in people using insulin, with a laboratory reference and glucose challenges.
  • iCGM clearance is the clearest public benchmark. It is also the gate for use with interoperable automated insulin delivery in the US.
  • PMA devices are not "below" iCGM. They went through a different, device-specific review. Check the SSED for range-specific accuracy.
  • TGA approval reflects device-specific evidence, but the threshold applied is not published. Check the indication on the ARTG entry (ages, adjunctive or non-adjunctive use).
  • Watch the population. Approval for children or type 1 diabetes may rest on data from adults or type 2 diabetes for some CE-marked devices (Diabetes UK, 2023).
  • Even under one standard, systems are not calibrated to the same reference. The same person can show different time in range on different sensors.
