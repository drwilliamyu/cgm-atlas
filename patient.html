Continuous glucose monitors

Information for people with diabetes and their families. Talk with your diabetes team before you choose or change a device.

What is a continuous glucose monitor?

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) is a small sensor that you wear on your skin. A thin, soft tip goes just under the skin. It measures glucose (sugar) in the fluid around your cells.

The sensor sends a new reading to your phone or reader every few minutes. It can warn you when your glucose goes too high or too low.

Glucose in this fluid changes a few minutes after glucose in your blood. When your glucose changes fast, for example after a meal or exercise, the CGM reading can be a little behind.

How accurate are they?

Researchers compare CGM readings with a laboratory blood test. The result is often given as a percentage. In this sheet we call it the average difference. Doctors call it MARD.

  • A smaller number means the CGM is, on average, closer to the blood test.
  • For example, 8% means that a reading of 10.0 mmol/L (180 mg/dL) is, on average, about 0.8 mmol/L (14 mg/dL) away from the blood test.
  • Each company tests its device in a different way. A difference of 1 or 2 points between two devices may not be important.
  • No CGM is perfect. If the reading does not match how you feel, check with a finger-prick test.

Questions to ask your diabetes team

  • Which devices can I get, and is there a subsidy for me?
  • Does the device work with my phone, or do I need a reader?
  • Does it work with my insulin pump, if I have one?
  • How often do I change the sensor, and where do I wear it?
  • When should I still do a finger-prick test?
  • What should I do if the sensor falls off or stops working?

This sheet is general information. It does not tell you which device to use. Your diabetes team will help you choose the device that suits you.
